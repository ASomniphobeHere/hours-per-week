The Shape of 24

A workshop exercise that spends twenty minutes building a participant’s week so it can take four hours a day back. The flow end to end, the instrument that carries it, and the eleven decisions that closed.

§7 · the instrument

One day, stacked

Everything the exercise does, it does to this object. A participant’s week rendered as a single day: a vertical stack of activity bands filling a 24-hour container, full-bleed, owning the whole viewport so it reads as a whole day rather than a chart of one.

The week is two days, not one — five workdays and two weekend days — so a toggle sits above the stack and each segment carries its own day’s occupied hours. Both totals are always live. That is what lets a weekend problem announce itself from the workday view.

Care

Rendered at 20 px/hour, roughly a 600 px phone viewport. Hours are the sketch’s: 23.7 occupied, 0.3 unallocated. Personal care (0.9 h) and Admin (0.6 h) show no label — below 20 px of band height the label is dropped, though both keep a full 44 px hit area. That is the type rule working, not a rendering fault.

Spine
Leftmost 8% of viewport width, the band’s hue at full saturation. At 320 px that is still 25 px.
Body
The remaining 92%, same hue at 12% opacity. Hue separates the bands; lightness cannot, because at 12% it collapses.
Ruler
One continuous scale over the spine, ticks at 0/3/…/24, each number on a translucent scrim so it holds against any hue.
Rim
The 24-hour line. One appearance, identical before and after breach. It is a rim, not an alert.
Stripes
45° red, 6 px period, clipped to everything below the rim — so Personal care, straddling the line, is striped only on its lower portion.

The three ways into S4

The flag is one room-scoped boolean, and it has to handle a room that is not in one state. All three paths carry the same five-second floor.

Force-advance from S1 uses whatever has been answered, unanswered gates treated as truthy, and every remaining field falling to its pack default. A partly-default schedule beats leaving someone out of the reveal — and the defaults are mandatory now, or the promise doesn’t hold.

§7.6 · §8.3 · the squeeze

What the stripes are for

The reveal is the whole exercise compressed into one gesture. Everything about how it renders is chosen to keep the participant looking at the stack rather than solving an arithmetic problem.

School goes on top, above sleep. Not appended at the bottom where it would read as optional. It displaces from the position of a fixed obligation, and everything below it moves down.

The squeeze is silent. No number tells you how much you are over. No toast, no error, no +3.7. You cut until the stripes stop. The rim rule does not change appearance at breach and the stripes do not animate in.

School only moves one way. 20 h weekly minimum, in 5 h steps upward. The decrement is disabled at the floor. Its sheet contains a stepper and nothing else — none of the questionnaire, because it is not something they told us about.

The participant cannot negotiate with the ask. They can only decide what it costs — and that decision, in the order they make it, is the finding.

The one numeric exception

The toggle segment’s hour count goes bold and red when its day breaches. On the day you are looking at, that duplicates the stripes deliberately: the number is already on screen and already changing, and leaving it neutral beside a striped stack reads as an oversight rather than as restraint. On the day you are not looking at, it is the only signal that exists — stripes cannot show you a stack that isn’t rendered.

It still never states the excess. 27.7 hr, not +3.7. The breach comes off a number they were already reading; the size of the problem comes off the striped region.

This is a departure from the 1.0 draft, which said the stripes were the entire signal and forbade any numeric overflow message anywhere. Both §7.6 and the acceptance criterion were rewritten rather than quietly failed.

When the squeeze doesn’t bite

Unallocated absorbs school before any band does. A participant with four or more hours of workday slack takes the reveal without breaching: no stripes, confirm enabled on arrival, cut order empty. That is a valid outcome and a real finding — their week had room — and the client neither marks it nor manufactures a breach to avoid it. Raising the ask to force a cut would make it a different ask for different people, and the shared moment is what the whole S3 hold exists to buy.

§7.7 · §7.8 · below the line

Zero is not a band

An activity at zero hours renders nothing. A zero-height band is a dead tap target participants will try to hit, and a band at some arbitrary minimum height would misrepresent the total.

Those activities live in Not included, below the 24-hour line, reached by scrolling. Muted, unhued, no spine, no hour count — this list is inventory, not part of the instrument. A section gated out at S1 and a section answered with zeros look identical; the route to zero is telemetry, not UI.

Tapping a row opens that activity’s sheet exactly as a band tap does, gate included and set to its falsy value. Give it hours and it slides into the stack at its pack order. At S4 the reverse is a legitimate move: cutting an activity to zero is how you solve the squeeze by removing something, and it logs as an hours.change to zero like any other reduction.

Unallocated is the arithmetic remainder, not an activity. Dashed outline, no fill, no spine, no tap target, and it disappears entirely once there is nothing left. It is also the reason leisure had to be a real activity with its own questions: if leisure were the residual, the exercise would be asking what is left over rather than what was chosen.

Discoverability is a footer count — 3 not included — that scrolls to the list. The stack is never shrunk to fit the list on screen; the stack owning the viewport is what makes it read as a full day.

§8.1 · correction

The sheet

Correction is a bottom sheet at 88% viewport height carrying that section’s screens, prefilled — stacked and scrollable rather than paged, because replay is review and paging through four screens to fix one number is worse than scrolling.

Because the sheet occludes the stack, its header shows the activity’s computed total, live, as fields change. The participant cannot see the consequence until dismissal, so the number stands in for the visual.

At the bottom sits the escape hatch: set directly flips the activity out of derivation and exposes plain numeric inputs for workday and weekend hours. It does not erase the underlying answers, and flipping back restores derivation from them. Constraints clamp silently — sleep stops at six hours, everything else at zero, and no error copy appears. The control simply stops.

§4 · content layer

Everything sayable is data

No question, no label, no activity, and no hue is compiled into the client. The pack is versioned, fetched at session start, validated on load, and can be replaced without a release.

That includes copy, in a register the spec polices tightly: plain, neutral, sentence case, no encouragement, and specifically no phrasing that implies a norm, a benchmark, or a comparison. A prompt that says most people underestimate this changes the answer and has no place in the instrument.

Three ways an activity gets its hours

ModeSourceWho set itRecoverable
derivedAn estimator over the answer map — either a fitted model or the plain arith.freqDuration pathThe system, by default—
directThe participant’s own numberThe participantNo — and deliberately so
fallbackA pack default, after the estimator threwThe system, under failureYes — retried each pass

The third mode is new in 1.1. Collapsing it into direct meant one dropped request pinned an activity to a default for the rest of the session, including after the participant edited the very inputs the estimator reads. A network failure is not a participant decision and must not be recorded as one.

Derived hours are never stored. They are recomputed from the flat answer map on every read, which is the invariant that makes question replay safe — editing one answer cannot leave a stale total anywhere in the system.

Household, and why it ships as coefficients

One activity is not arithmetic on its own answers. Household comes from a model fitted against the ATUS time-use extract, taking demographic and household inputs. It ships as closed-form coefficients inside the pack, evaluated client-side.

That is a decision about the room rather than about model quality. Forty phones on venue wifi, a network call inside a twenty-question flow, and a fallback rule that catches every failure with a default — a remote estimator degrades exactly when the workshop is busiest. estimators/ is therefore build tooling: it fits offline and emits coefficients, and none of it reaches the client.

If the model turns out not to reduce to a closed form, that is first a reason to reconsider the model, and only then a reason to reconsider this.

§6.2 · operator tooling

One screen, one button

The console exists to answer a single question — has enough of the room finished? — and to act on the answer. It holds no local state; everything on screen came from the last poll.

Join code large and permanent, so a latecomer can be onboarded without navigating away. ready / total in the largest type on the page. Five stage counts beneath it. Values swap on the three-second poll with no transition animation, because a count that tweens is a count that can be misread mid-flight.

On poll failure the numbers stay, dimmed, with a small reconnecting note — the exact opposite of the participant’s hold screen, and deliberately so. A stale hold screen worries someone for nothing; a stale console misleads someone who is about to make a decision from the numbers on it.

The button arms rather than fires, and the armed label restates total rather than ready, because the flag pulls in the seventeen who have not finished too. A press at 3/40 should look wrong at the moment of confirming it. It is not gated on any threshold: waiting is a facilitation judgement.

Unauthenticated by decision, not by oversight. The exposure is one boolean, in one room, for one supervised session, and the only thing protecting it is that the roomId never leaves the facilitator’s browser. A second facilitator, a persistent room, or an unsupervised session all change that calculus.

§10 · the output

What the debrief actually reads

The debrief is the point of the exercise and telemetry is the product. Everything else in the system exists to produce two fields, and if a build decision trades against them, the fields win.

Per-activity deltacomplete − finishWhat the ask cost, activity by activity

School above minimumweekly > 20 at completeSomeone who chose to spend more than the ask
Read cut order against slack at finish. Unallocated absorbs school before any band does, and that absorption emits no event. So the first logged cut is not the first real loss for anyone who had slack — it is the first loss they had to make a decision about. Both readings are useful and they are not the same reading. A debrief that quotes first cut without slack at finish is quoting a participant who may have already given up two hours silently.

Three snapshots carry the rest: end of S1, at Finish before the reveal, and at complete after the rebalance. The middle one is what makes per-activity delta a measurement of the ask rather than of the whole session.
